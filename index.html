Why This Exists

Anyone can run an ad for a jersey. Only Quarter Athletic can back it up with the truth — and that truth is the actual strategy.

The problem

Every piece of QA marketing to date has been filtered through one true but incomplete lens: a company that makes apparel. That's real. It's also the smallest true thing about the business. Real collegiate licensing, team-curated identity work, 10% of every sale reinvested back into the program, a growing multi-sport roster — none of that has ever shown up in what a coach sees on Meta.

Left as-is, that gap has a cost: if the only thing a coach can evaluate is price and turnaround time, QA is competing on the one axis a print shop can always match. Nothing can match the founder's story, the licensing, or the 10% back — but only if someone actually says it out loud.

The approach

This isn't inventing a new brand. It's sequencing the truth QA already has, in the order every brand that's earned real loyalty has always used: prove it's real, prove it's yours, prove it gives back, prove it stays, prove it's bigger than one sport. Skip a step and the next one doesn't land — nobody believes “partner for life” from a company they don't yet trust is legitimate.

The sport rollout follows the same logic. Lacrosse and baseball don't launch cold in month one competing with rugby's proof — each enters inside the month that makes it most believable: lacrosse through identity, baseball through give-back.

The purpose isn't better-looking ads for their own sake. It's more coaches requesting quotes, at a lower cost per lead over time, because the brand has already done part of the convincing before the price sheet ever comes up — for rugby, and for the two sports QA is still earning the right to be trusted in.

How brands actually grow

Nike didn't become Nike by running one campaign forever, and it didn't get there by changing its logo every season either. It moved through stages — credibility, identity, community, loyalty, culture — keeping the swoosh and the voice fixed while the story evolved. This roadmap runs Quarter Athletic through the same five stages, using QA's own facts and its real sport priorities instead of borrowed ones.

How this toolkit works

Three things stay constant everywhere in this document, so nothing below needs to repeat them:

One concept, three ratios

Every concept keeps the same subject, lighting, and mood across 1080×1080, 1080×1350, and 1080×1920 — only the framing and the reserved text space change. Same rule the rugby shoot already used.

Reskin before reinventing

When a concept works, it moves to the next sport as a reskin — same composition and lighting instructions, different kit/equipment. Month 4 is written this way on purpose: one prompt set, three sports.

Sports earn their way in

Rugby is live every month. Lacrosse and baseball are staged in deliberately — identity month for lacrosse, community month for baseball — instead of both launching cold in month one.

Zones are fixed, copy isn't

Each month below ends with a scaled layout showing exactly where headline, tagline, image, and logo go at all three ratios. Drop that month's copy in and the composition holds — logo width stays fixed (22% square / 26% portrait / 30% story) regardless of concept.

The hierarchy strategy: what's fixed, what moves

Every ad, every month, every sport runs through the same three-layer typography — Hero Statement, Power Statement, Supporting Copy — and the same visual system. That consistency is what makes five monthly moods and three sports still read as one brand.

Never changes, any month or sport

  • QA logo & jersey crest — always the real file, never AI-modified or reinterpreted
  • Typography: three-layer hierarchy, Inter for the text pass
  • Invisible grid — margins, alignment, one focal point
  • Ghost-mannequin production system — never real photography for product shots
  • Voice floor: never “cheap,” “affordable,” “budget,” “lowest price”

Rotates monthly

  • Lead accent color, pulled from the fixed palette (navy → magenta → green → gold → full range)
  • Which sport(s) are live that month
  • Emotional register — truth, identity, community, loyalty, culture
  • Lighting mood — dawn/dusk quiet → golden-hour → bright midday → matched multi-era light → wide architectural
  • Headline theme & the specific proof point behind it

Why This Exists

Recruiting is broken by noise. Twenty Five Select is what recruiting should have been all along — but only if the marketing proves the verification instead of just claiming it.

The problem

Twenty Five Select launched into a category full of noise — fake profiles, inflated stats, DM spam, black-box matching. The product's answer is real: 100% ID verification, human admin review, a match score that explains itself. None of that has a track record yet — the Hall of Fame page still reads “First commitments coming soon.” Marketing that oversells results a platform hasn't earned yet becomes exactly the kind of noise this platform exists to remove.

It's also a two-sided marketplace before it has density on either side — every athlete needs enough real coaches browsing to be worth paying for, and every coach needs enough verified athletes to be worth signing up for free.

The approach

So this arc doesn't sell outcomes — it sells the verification process, month by month, to both sides of the marketplace at once. Athletes and coaches never share an ad; each side gets its own proof, in its own accent color, because they're not making the same decision.

Every fact used here is already true today: the merch tie-in with Quarter Athletic, the free-forever coach plan, the founder personally answering corporate inquiries. Nothing here is aspirational copy waiting to become true — it's already-true copy that just hasn't been said yet.

The purpose isn't more downloads. It's a marketplace with enough real density on both sides to be self-sustaining — verified athletes worth a coach's time, and enough free coach accounts that paying to be seen by them is worth it. Everything below earns that balance honestly, instead of faking it with results that don't exist yet.

How trust-layer platforms earn it

Twenty Five Select isn't a jersey brand chasing culture — it's a verification layer chasing trust between two strangers, athlete and coach. That's a different growth shape than Quarter Athletic's, closer to the platforms that made stranger-to-stranger trust feel safe and boring instead of risky.
